# Findings

What this benchmark has established, what it falsified, and what it learned
about measuring itself. `RESULTS.md` holds the numbers and is generated by
`--summarise`; this file holds the reasoning, which a table cannot carry.

Campaign of 2026-08-19/20, on `prime` / `test-0.1` (280 queries, k=20),
metrics from the official `stark_qa` evaluator.

---

## 1. Relational text is worth a great deal — through the lexical channel

**The finding.** Two arms, same embedding model (`qwen3-embedding-0.6b`),
same ~1.0 chunks/node, differing only in whether the indexed document carries
a `- relations:` block naming the node's neighbours:

| agent | no relations | with relations | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| dense | 0.18274 | 0.18664 | **+2%** |
| lexical | 0.20479 | 0.24913 | **+22%** |
| hybrid | 0.19870 | **0.28214** | **+42%** |
| rerank | not run | **0.41948** | — |

`qwen-rel-whole` + `rerank` at **0.41948 mrr / 0.35357 hit@1** is the best
figure this project has produced — against `vss-control`'s best of 0.23111
and STaRK's published ada-002 VSS figure of 0.2350.

**The mechanism, which was not predicted.** PRIME's queries name related
entities verbatim: *"a drug that targets X and is indicated for Y"*. Those
names appear in the answer's own document **only** in the relations version.
BM25 matches them directly; a single dense vector compresses them away. So
nearly the whole gain arrives through the lexical channel, and dense barely
moves.

**Retrodiction it explains.** Across the older arms, `lexical` beat `dense`
on 5 of 9 configs, which read as noise. It was this effect showing through
weakly on corpora that lacked the relational text — and the clearest
exception was `vss-control`, which has the largest dense margin in the table.

**What follows for the architecture.** A knowledge graph's edges are worth
more as *text in the index* than as a traversal at query time, at least on
this benchmark, and worth most to the retrievers that can match a name
exactly. That is a result about how to use a graph, not about how good the
graph is.

## 2. The hypothesis this campaign falsified

`qwen-wholedoc` dense (0.183) against `vss-control` dense (0.231) was read
here as **"ada-002 is reading a richer corpus"** — the theory being that
STaRK's precomputed vectors are over `add_rel=True` documents while every
local arm embedded `add_rel=False` text.

That theory redirected a day of endpoint time. It is **wrong**. Giving qwen
the relational text moved dense by **0.004**, leaving the gap to ada-002
essentially intact.

So on the dense channel, ada-002 is simply better here than
`qwen3-embedding-0.6b`, with one standing caveat: **every local number is for
a Q8_0 GGUF served by llama.cpp**, not for the model as published. See
`B-ADA002-PROVENANCE-1` for what remains unverified about STaRK's own
embeddings; it no longer changes any conclusion of ours.

**The arm was built to test a hypothesis, the hypothesis lost, and the arm
produced the project's best result anyway** — for a mechanism nobody
predicted. That is the case for building the cell rather than arguing about
it.

## 3. `boundary-preference` is an outlier, now on three models

redstring's own default chunker came **last on dense retrieval** on Nemotron,
and again on qwen's mini set. `sliding-1000-500`, at more than double the
granularity, beat it both times. `RESULTS.md` finding 4 previously ruled out
the two obvious explanations — not lost content, not chunk length.

Three models agreeing points at the chunker rather than at an interaction
with one embedding model. This is the most concrete thing this benchmark has
found *about redstring*, and it deserves a passage-level diagnostic rather
than another arm.

Not yet tested on `prime-rel`, deliberately: re-testing a three-time loser
was judged lower value than measuring the winner on the corpus that matters.

## 4. What we learned about measuring this system

Every entry here cost real time, and each is a trap that will be walked into
again by anyone who does not know it.

### A snapshot cannot distinguish stalled from busy

A healthy ingest was diagnosed as hung on four signals that all looked
damning and were all misreads:

| signal | why it was wrong |
|---|---|
| log had stopped | `ingest done` prints *before* the 8.1M-edge phase, which logs nothing (`B-EDGE-PROGRESS-1`) |
| chunk count flat over 30s | chunks commit in `CHUNK_BATCH = 1000` steps; on long documents a flush is ~6 minutes apart |
| client CPU unchanged over 20s | an I/O-bound client accrues under a second in 20s |
| `/slots` showed `processed: 0` | that is what a slot reports the instant it picks up a task |

What settled it: `id_task` climbing across three samples, and a 3-minute
window showing +5,010 chunks. **Only a window measures a rate.**

### Extrapolate by characters, not documents — and even that has a caveat

A `--limit 3000` calibration implied a 6.5-hour arm; the truth was ~2. The
head of `prime-rel` averages **5,278 chars** against the corpus mean of
**1,761**, and `--limit` always samples the head.

The engine's own ETA has this bug: it counts nodes, so it read 152 → 180 →
**198 minutes** while the arm was 11% through its nodes and **28.9% through
its characters** (`B-RATE-UNIT-1`).

But characters are not a constant rate either. Attention is quadratic in
sequence length, so a 1,024-document slice averaging 5,278 chars measured
1.94M chars/min while whole `prime-rel` sustained ~720k
(`B-QUADRATIC-DOCS-1`).

### Assert on the data, not on exit codes

Every check that caught something real this campaign was a data check:

- `total_chars = 227,851,132` — **exactly 100.00%** of the corpus, proving the
  rel arm splits rather than truncates
- `edges = 8,100,498` — exactly `edges.jsonl`'s line count
- `nomic-wholedoc` and `qwen-wholedoc` both score lexical **0.20479**,
  identical to five decimals, because BM25 does not see the embedding model —
  an unplanned confirmation that tenant isolation is clean
- a warm-cache arm scoring `0.3590479185937771`, byte-identical to its
  pre-cache figure

And one CLAUDE.md warning that was **stale**: `self_loops_dropped: 0` is the
correct answer for the current PRIME export, which contains no self-loops in
any of its 8,100,498 edges.

### The helper works, nobody calls it

Shipped twice in one session historically, and guarded against twice more
this campaign. Deleting `embedding_cache=` from the CLI call site leaves
**all 212 other tests green**; only an AST check on the call site catches it.
Same for the `enable_thinking: False` flag, which was protected purely by a
library default and had no test at all.

**Break the implementation on purpose and watch the suite go red.** Every
test added this campaign was verified that way, including two that caught a
vector/text misalignment — the defect that fills a store and scores like
noise.

## 5. Cost, and what it bought

| | |
|---|---|
| ingest, `prime-rel` whole-document | 61 min, 129,375 nodes, 100.00% of text |
| edge load, 8.1M relationships | ~28 min, silent |
| `dense` / `hybrid` / `lexical` scoring | 1-5 min each |
| `rerank` scoring | ~35 min, 1 LLM call/query, **0 extract failures** |

**The embedding cache** (`kg_embedding_cache`, keyed on
`(model, document_prefix, sha256(text))`) measured on `prime-mini`:

| case | wall | hit rate | speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold | 293.8 s | 1.7% | — |
| same config again | **14.1 s** | 100% | **21x** |
| different chunker | 409.3 s | **44.7%** | ~1.45x |

The third row corrected an estimate of mine that was too rosy. I predicted a
three-chunker sweep would cost roughly one endpoint pass, reasoning from 86%
of `prime` being under 1,000 characters. **Hit rate is per chunk, not per
document**: a finer chunker emits 2.43 chunks/node against 1.20, and every
extra chunk is a guaranteed miss.

Where it pays enormously is re-running the *same* configuration — crash
recovery, re-scoring after a code change, re-ingesting a tenant.

## 6. Bugs found in redstring and in this harness

Finding these is a legitimate output of this project.

1. **`WholeDocumentChunker` ignored `max_chunk_size`** (this repo, fixed).
   The ingest engine's re-split exists so a character cap need not be guessed
   right; against a chunker that ignores the cap it halves four times,
   re-chunks identically, and raises the provider's error instead of the real
   one. It was a **guaranteed no-op for the chunker that most needs it**,
   because the arms using whole documents are exactly the arms feeding a
   context-limited server. Killed a 46-minute ingest on `prime-rel`'s
   133,778-character outlier. The engine now also raises immediately if a
   halving fails to shrink anything.
2. **`BoundaryPreferenceChunker` degenerated to 1-character advances**
   (redstring PR #64, merged). Found by looking at the data — "does any
   document justify 1,018 chunks averaging 147 characters?"
3. **`SlidingWindowChunker` emits one fully-redundant tail chunk** per
   document longer than the window whenever overlap > 0
   (`B-SLIDING-REDUNDANT-1`).
4. **`EmbeddingProvider` could not express task prefixes.** Design gap, since
   closed by redstring ADR 0043.

## 7. What is worth doing next

1. **A chunked arm on `prime-rel`** (`sliding-1000-500`). Everything on the
   rel corpus so far is whole-document, which deliberately neutralises
   chunking — so it measures the *model* and almost nothing about redstring.
   This is the cell that measures redstring's chunker on the corpus that
   matters.
2. **`rerank` at `fetch=40`.** It currently fetches exactly `k`, making it a
   pure ordering experiment whose ceiling is hybrid's recall@20 of 0.46508 —
   and it converted that into 0.41948 mrr, which is efficient enough to
   suggest the ceiling is now the binding constraint.
3. **MAG**, the corpus-generalisation cell. ~849M chars, ~7h locally; this is
   the case where renting GPUs actually pays, and only the embedding call
   needs to move — chunking, Postgres, Neo4j and scoring are all local and
   fast.
4. **A backend re-baseline on TEI or vLLM**, so numbers are for the model
   rather than for a Q8_0 GGUF served by a text-generation engine. Costs a
   re-ingest of every arm, so it belongs with the MAG work rather than
   mid-campaign.
